The job, and what to type

Use only your own marks and the topic of each question. Not the question paper, the questions themselves, the answers, or anyone else's marks. The marks below are a sample from a Data Structures mock, made up for this demo: 10 questions, 5 marks each, each question tagged with its topic. Type your own marks and topic names in their place. 

Here are the marks from my marked mock test (Data Structures, 10 questions, 5 marks each). Each question has its topic.
Q1 Arrays 5/5
Q2 Linked lists 2/5
Q3 Stacks 4/5
Q4 Queues 5/5
Q5 Trees 1/5
Q6 Linked lists 3/5
Q7 Hashing 2/5
Q8 Trees 2/5
Q9 Sorting 4/5
Q10 Hashing 3/5

- Add up my marks for each topic and show each as marks out of that topic's total, with a percentage.
- Rank the topics from weakest to strongest by percentage.
- Tell me my 3 weakest topics.
- Use only these marks. Don't guess why I lost marks.
- Give me a 5-day revision plan, 2 hours a day, with the most time on the weakest topics.

Why these lines

One question per line, with its topic, is what lets it add up a topic that appears more than once. "Marks out of that topic's total, with a percentage" matters because raw marks mislead: Linked lists scored 5, the same as Arrays, but out of 10, not 5. Ranking "by percentage" puts the weak topics at the top for the right reason. "Use only these marks. Don't guess why I lost marks" stops it inventing reasons it can't know from a list of numbers. And the plan line says how much time you really have: 5 days, 2 hours a day.

How it was tested

One run on 9 November 2026, on free ChatGPT in a web browser, not signed in, in a new chat, so there was no account, memory or saved chat history to draw on. The model in use wasn't shown. The six checks, the sample marks, the right answers and the prompt were written down and saved before the prompt was sent.

  1. Every topic total is right: Arrays 5/5, Linked lists 5/10, Stacks 4/5, Queues 5/5, Trees 3/10, Hashing 5/10, Sorting 4/5, and 31/50 overall if it gives one.
  2. Every percentage is right: 100, 50, 80, 100, 30, 50 and 80.
  3. It ranks by percentage, not raw marks: Trees weakest, then Linked lists and Hashing (tied), and no topic scored 5 out of 5 is put level with them because it also has 5 marks.
  4. The 3 weakest topics it names are Trees, Linked lists and Hashing.
  5. Only the marks: it doesn't invent reasons for lost marks or say what the questions were about.
  6. The plan is 5 days of 2 hours, 10 hours in all; Trees gets the most time, and Linked lists and Hashing each get more than any topic at 80% or above.

What came back

The sums were right, every one: each topic's marks out of its own total, every percentage, and 31 out of 50, 62%, overall. It ranked by percentage: Trees at 30% first, then Linked Lists and Hashing at 50%, with a note: "Topics with equal percentages share the same performance level." Arrays, also on 5 marks but out of 5, came out strongest, not level with the weak two. The 3 weakest it named were Trees, Linked Lists and Hashing, which is right. It said "No assumptions about why marks were lost", and kept to that.

The days of the plan were sound too. Five days of 2 hours, 10 hours in all. Trees gets all of day 1 and an hour of day 2, about 3 hours. Linked Lists and Hashing get about 2 hours each. Stacks and Sorting get half an hour each on day 4, and day 5 is mixed revision and a short timed mock. The study tasks it named for each day, like practising tree traversal, are general revision advice, not claims about what the mock asked.

Five of the six checks passed.

The catch

The miss is one box. Right under "Total study time: 10 hours (2 hours per day)." the answer has a "Time allocation" box: Trees 4 hours, Linked Lists 3, Hashing 3, Stacks & Sorting 2, Arrays & Queues 2. The box prints no total, but its five lines add up to 14 hours, not 10. And it doesn't match its own days: the days give Trees about 3 hours, the box says 4.

It's an easy one to miss, because the box is the neatest part of the answer, with coloured bars, and it sits right under the line that says 10 hours. Plan your week from the box and you've planned four hours that don't exist. Follow the days instead, and add up any summary before you trust it.

The stamp

Half works · 9 Nov 2026 · free ChatGPT, in a browser · 5 of 6 checks passed. It's good at turning a marked mock into a weak-topic list: right totals, right percentages, a ranking by percentage, the right 3 weak topics, and no guessing. The 10-hour day plan is sound. But its time box adds up to 14 hours under a 10-hour heading. Add up the hours yourself. One run is one run: try it with your own marks and see what yours does.
