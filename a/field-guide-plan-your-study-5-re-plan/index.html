The plan slipped: re-plan in 5 minutes.

Plan your study (medical students), part 5: two lost days of a revision week re-planned with your own limits, tested on free ChatGPT.

15 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · sources read up to 14 Nov 2026

A revision week rarely goes to plan. Two days go by, and the choice seems to be cramming the lost hours into the rest of the week or giving up on it. There's a third option: re-plan the days that are left, with your own limits, and find out first whether it still fits. This is part 5 of the Field Guide series on planning your study as a medical student: one job, tested on a real screen, with the result said plainly.

The job, and what to type

Part 4 split the hours by your weak subjects. This part rescues a week that slipped. The plan below is a sample, made up for this demo: four subject names and hours a day, nothing from inside a subject. Type your own plan in its place: subject names and hours only, no name or roll number.

The sample week has 18 hours, Sunday off. Monday and Tuesday held 6 of them, and both days went by with no revision. That leaves Wednesday to Sunday. With at most 4 hours a day and 2 on Sunday, those five days hold exactly 18 hours: just enough, with nothing to spare.

The prompt, word for word: 

I'm an MBBS student. This was my revision plan for this week, in hours a day, subject level only:
- Mon: Pathology 2, Pharmacology 1
- Tue: Microbiology 2, Forensic Medicine 1
- Wed: Pathology 1, Pharmacology 2
- Thu: Microbiology 1, Forensic Medicine 2
- Fri: Pathology 2, Microbiology 1
- Sat: Pharmacology 2, Forensic Medicine 1
- Sun: rest
Weekly hours: Pathology 5, Pharmacology 5, Microbiology 4, Forensic Medicine 4. Total 18.

The plan slipped. Monday and Tuesday are gone: I did no revision on either day. Re-plan the rest of the week:
- Only Wednesday to Sunday are left.
- Every subject still gets its full weekly hours.
- No more than 4 hours on any day, and at most 2 hours on Sunday.
- Whole hours only.
- Show a table: day, subjects with hours, day total. Then the total for each subject.
- If it can't all fit, say so plainly and tell me which hours to drop, instead of squeezing them in.
- Subject level only. No topic lists.

The 4-hour cap, the 2-hour Sunday and the 18 hours are the sample's numbers. Use yours.

How it was tested

One run on 15 November 2026, on free ChatGPT in a web browser, not signed in, in a temporary chat: no memory, no past chats. The page didn't say which AI model answered. OpenAI's model release notes say its 29 October GPT-6 update applies to people using ChatGPT without signing in [2]. ChatGPT is for ages 13 and up, and under-18s need a parent's or guardian's permission [1].

Six checks were written down, with an answer key, before the prompt was sent:

  1. Monday and Tuesday get no hours; the new plan uses only Wednesday to Sunday.
  2. Every subject keeps its hours: Pathology 5, Pharmacology 5, Microbiology 4, Forensic Medicine 4, 18 in all.
  3. No day over 4 hours, Sunday 2 at most, whole hours only.
  4. Every day total and subject total it prints adds up.
  5. It says it fits (4 days at 4 hours plus Sunday at 2 is exactly 18) and drops nothing.
  6. Subject level only, and no health, sleep or medicine advice.

What came back

It opened with the verdict: "Fits exactly — no hours need to be dropped." Then: "You have 18 hours remaining and 18 hours of available capacity from Wednesday to Sunday."

The new week: Wednesday Pathology 2, Pharmacology 2; Thursday Microbiology 2, Forensic Medicine 2; Friday Pathology 2, Microbiology 1, Forensic Medicine 1; Saturday Pathology 1, Pharmacology 2, Forensic Medicine 1; Sunday Pharmacology 1, Microbiology 1. Added by hand, the subjects come to 5, 5, 4 and 4, the days to 4, 4, 4, 4 and 2. It printed the same totals, and a list of the limits it had kept. All 6 checks passed.

Worth a look

Every day from Wednesday to Sunday is now at its limit. That is the right answer for this sample, because the room left was exactly the hours needed. But it means the new plan has no slack: if one more day goes, it won't fit. Leave yourself a spare hour if your limits allow it, or decide now which hours you'd drop first.

The stamp

Works · 15 Nov 2026 · free ChatGPT, not signed in · 6 of 6 checks. It said plainly that the week still fits, kept every subject's hours and every limit, and got every sum right. One run is one run, on a made-up sample.

Before you start

For revising only. Never in an exam, and for marked work, follow your college's AI rules. Type your own plan, give your limits in plain words, and keep it at subject level.
