Open every source.

Field Guide, Don't get fooled by AI, part 1 of 6. Free ChatGPT gave a link for every UPI fact and every fact was right, but its first link was a dead page.

11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · sources read up to 12 Nov 2026

A link next to every fact makes an answer look checked, as if someone already did the work. It's easy to stop checking at that point and forward it on. This is part 1 of "Don't get fooled by AI", a new Field Guide series: six quick checks to run before you believe or pass on what an AI tells you, one check per part, each tested on a real screen. Part 1 is the simplest one: open every source.

The job, and what to type

The question had to be easy to check and come with real numbers: when UPI started in India, and how many UPI payments were made last month. UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is India's system for paying from your phone, bank to bank. NPCI, the National Payments Corporation of India, runs it and publishes the monthly counts [1]. 

Search the web and answer with sources: when did UPI start in India, and how many UPI payments were made last month? Give a link for every fact.

"Give a link for every fact" is the line that matters. It makes the answer show its working, so every claim has somewhere you can go and look. The test is whether those places say what the answer says.

How it was tested

One run on 10 November 2026, at about 10:24 in the morning, on free ChatGPT in a web browser, not signed in, in a new chat, so there was no account, memory or saved chat history to draw on. It searched the web by itself and answered in under 20 seconds. The model in use wasn't shown on screen. OpenAI's own release notes, in their 29 October 2026 entry, say free ChatGPT without signing in runs on GPT-6 [5]. The five checks were written down and saved before the question was sent. Then four of the five links were opened by hand, between about 10:27 and 10:32, and read; the fifth, Mint's, wasn't.

  1. It gives a link for every fact it states.
  2. Every link opens a real page: no dead link, no "page not found", no made-up address.
  3. Each linked page says the fact it is attached to, with the same dates and numbers.
  4. Last month's count is for the latest month NPCI has published, and matches NPCI's own UPI statistics page.
  5. The counts come from NPCI itself, or from a page that names NPCI as its source.

What came back

A tidy answer with a link next to everything. UPI started as a pilot on 11 April 2016 with 21 banks, and opened to the public on 25 August 2016, with three links under "Sources". For last month it gave October 2026: 25.47 billion UPI payments (2,546.67 crore), worth Rs 31.53 lakh crore, with 758 banks live, linked to NPCI's own statistics page. It added that September had 24.07 billion, credited to a Mint report of 1 November 2026.

Every fact was right. NPCI's table, opened on 10 November, shows October 2026 as the latest month: 25,466.70 million payments, Rs 31,52,929.46 crore and 758 banks; September shows 24,068.38 million [1]. The Indian Economic Service's page gives the pilot with 21 member banks on 11 April 2016 and public use from 25 August 2016 [2]. A government press release from 30 April 2026 says UPI was launched on 11 April 2016 [3]. One number, "82.2 crore" payments a day, isn't on NPCI's page: it's ChatGPT's own sum, 2,546.67 crore over October's 31 days, and the sum is right.

Three of the five checks passed: a link for every fact, last month's count right, and the count from NPCI itself.

The catch

The first link under "Sources" is called "NPCI – UPI milestones". It points at NPCI's own website, which makes it look like the best source of all. It opens NPCI's "Page Not Found", with or without the tracking tag ChatGPT added to the address. The page says nothing about when UPI started.

The dates happened to be right, and the other two links backed them up. But that first link proved nothing, and nothing in the answer said so. Someone who opened only the first link would have found a dead end; someone who opened none would never know. Whether that page ever existed can't be told from a "Page Not Found". What matters is that it wasn't there when it was opened.

Every link, opened

The answer gave five different links (the NPCI statistics link appears twice). Four were opened by hand on 10 November 2026; the fifth, Mint's, wasn't.

  • "NPCI – UPI milestones": NPCI's own "Page Not Found". Says nothing.
  • "Indian Economic Service – UPI history": says the pilot launch was on 11 April 2016 with 21 member banks, and public use from 25 August 2016 [2].
  • "Government of India – UPI's 10-year milestone": a press release from 30 April 2026 that says UPI was launched on 11 April 2016 [3].
  • "NPCI – Official UPI monthly statistics": October 2026, 25,466.70 million payments, Rs 31,52,929.46 crore, 758 banks; September, 24,068.38 million [1].
  • "Mint's report of 1 November 2026": not opened. The news site wasn't allowed in the browser used for this test, so the Mint page itself was not checked. The 24.07 billion the answer credits to Mint does match NPCI's table [1].

The stamp

Half works · 10 Nov 2026 · free ChatGPT, in a browser · 3 of 5 checks passed. Every fact in the answer was right, and the counts came from NPCI itself. But the answer looked fully sourced, and one of its sources wasn't there. Open every link before you repeat a fact, and trust the fact only when the page you opened says it. No page, or a page that doesn't say it: don't pass the fact on yet. One run is one run: try the same question and open what yours gives you.

Before you use it

ChatGPT is for ages 13 and over, or the minimum age in your country, and under 18 needs a parent's or guardian's permission [4].
